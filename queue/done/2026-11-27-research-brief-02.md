Most hospitals in Pakistan had the medicines to save mothers and newborns. Very few had the written guidelines for using them.

A national assessment of 275 public and private hospitals across 141 districts, published in the Journal of Global Health in April 2026, checked whether facilities were ready to deliver life-saving maternal and newborn care.

What stood out:
– Oxytocin for bleeding after birth was available in 83.3% of facilities, but guidelines in only 17.9%
– Bag-and-mask for newborn resuscitation in 66.8%, guidelines in 8.8%
– Antenatal steroids for preterm babies in 57.7%, guidelines in 10.2%
– Only 13.8% had a kangaroo mother care unit and 18.5% a neonatologist
– Just 1 of 275 offered all 11 maternal emergency functions

Balochistan and Gilgit-Baltistan were furthest behind. Sindh and Punjab did better on equipment, but guidelines were thin everywhere.

Worth keeping in mind: the data were collected in 2022, it's a one-time snapshot, and some answers were self-reported by facility staff.

The authors also call for stronger monitoring and health information systems to prevent stock-outs and guide resources. Data only helps when it reaches the people who allocate them.

From your experience, what's the bigger gap at facility level: supplies, trained staff, or clear protocols?

Source: Mustufa MA, Ariff S, et al. Journal of Global Health, 2026;16:04103 (April 2026)
